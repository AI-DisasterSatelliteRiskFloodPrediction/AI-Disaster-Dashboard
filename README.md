## 📌 Project Overview

**AI-Disaster-Dashboard** is an Artificial Intelligence and Earth Observation research and technology prototype developed to support **disaster risk reduction, flood monitoring, climate resilience, and geospatial decision support**.

The project integrates:

* 🛰️ Satellite Earth Observation
* 🤖 Artificial Intelligence (AI)
* 🧠 Machine Learning (ML)
* 🌍 Geospatial analysis
* ☁️ Google Earth Engine
* 📊 Interactive data visualization
* 🚨 Flood-risk assessment and decision-support concepts

The project transforms satellite-derived information into interpretable disaster-risk information to support researchers, disaster-management stakeholders, and decision-makers.

The primary use case focuses on **flood monitoring and disaster-risk assessment in Rwanda**, with potential application to other climate-vulnerable regions across Africa.

> **Project status:** Research and technology prototype — 2026

---

## 🎯 Research Objective

### General Objective

To develop an **AI-driven satellite image analysis framework** that supports national disaster risk reduction through improved flood detection, risk assessment, geospatial visualization, and decision support.

### Specific Objectives

1. To process satellite Earth Observation data for flood monitoring.
2. To investigate Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 satellite imagery for flood-related analysis.
3. To develop AI/ML-based approaches for identifying potential flood-risk areas.
4. To classify flood risk into interpretable categories.
5. To develop an interactive dashboard for communicating disaster-risk information.
6. To establish a reproducible workflow for satellite-based disaster monitoring.
7. To explore the potential of AI and Earth Observation technologies for strengthening climate resilience in Africa.

---

## 🔬 Research Problem

Flooding is a major environmental and disaster-risk challenge affecting communities, infrastructure, agriculture, ecosystems, and economic activities.

Satellite Earth Observation provides valuable information for monitoring environmental changes over large geographic areas. However, transforming satellite data into timely, understandable, and actionable information for disaster-risk decision-making remains a significant challenge.

This project addresses this challenge by developing an integrated workflow connecting:

**Satellite Earth Observation → Geospatial Processing → Flood Detection → AI/ML Analysis → Risk Classification → Decision Support**

The prototype is intended to demonstrate how satellite data and artificial intelligence can contribute to improved disaster-risk monitoring and climate resilience.

---

# 🛰️ Research Methodology

The project follows an integrated Earth Observation, Artificial Intelligence, and geospatial analysis workflow.

### 1. Satellite Data Acquisition

The research investigates satellite Earth Observation datasets, including:

* **Sentinel-1 Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)**
* **Sentinel-2 Multispectral Imagery**

Sentinel-1 is particularly useful for flood monitoring because radar observations can provide information under cloudy conditions.

Sentinel-2 provides multispectral information useful for water detection, environmental monitoring, land-cover analysis, and complementary flood assessment.

---

### 2. Google Earth Engine Processing

Google Earth Engine is used as a geospatial processing environment for satellite-data analysis.

The workflow can include:

* Image collection
* Temporal filtering
* Spatial filtering
* Cloud and data-quality considerations
* Image preprocessing
* Spectral and radar analysis
* Feature extraction
* Flood-related indicators
* Risk-map generation

**Google Earth Engine Script:**

https://code.earthengine.google.com/289532d6c0d03325fb10a7429d5b203d

> Access to the Earth Engine script may require appropriate Google Earth Engine permissions.

---

### 3. Flood Detection

The prototype investigates satellite-derived indicators for identifying areas potentially affected by flooding.

Depending on the dataset and analysis approach, flood-related information may be derived from:

* Radar backscatter changes
* Water extent
* Spectral characteristics
* Temporal changes
* Vegetation and environmental indicators
* Geospatial contextual variables

The resulting information can be used as input for further risk analysis.

---

### 4. AI and Machine Learning

Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning approaches are investigated to support the transformation of satellite-derived information into interpretable disaster-risk information.

The research framework may incorporate:

* Satellite-derived features
* Environmental variables
* Flood indicators
* Feature extraction
* Machine-learning classification
* Flood probability estimation
* Risk-level classification

The AI/ML component is intended to support research into automated and scalable disaster-risk analysis.

---

### 5. Flood Risk Classification

The prototype uses an interpretable risk framework:

| Risk Level         | Interpretation                                     |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 🔴 **High Risk**   | Areas with comparatively high estimated flood risk |
| 🟠 **Medium Risk** | Areas with moderate estimated flood risk           |
| 🟢 **Low Risk**    | Areas with comparatively low estimated flood risk  |

Risk thresholds and model parameters can be refined through additional validation, historical-event analysis, and field-based evidence.

The dashboard is designed to present:

* AI/ML analysis concepts
* Geospatial visualization

* Disaster-risk indicators

# ⚠️ Limitations

The current project is a research and technology prototype and has limitations.

These include:

* Limited availability of comprehensive ground observations
* Satellite-data availability and quality constraints
* Model uncertainty
* Need for additional historical-event validation
* Need for field-based validation
* Need for operational testing
* Need for further calibration of risk thresholds

Therefore, prototype outputs should be interpreted as **research and decision-support information** and should not be treated as a replacement for official emergency warnings or national disaster-management systems.
