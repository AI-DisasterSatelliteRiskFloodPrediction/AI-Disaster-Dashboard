## 📌 1. Project Overview

**AI-Disaster-Dashboard** is a research and technology prototype focused on exploring how satellite Earth Observation, rainfall information, geospatial analysis, and Artificial Intelligence can contribute to flood monitoring and disaster risk reduction in Rwanda.

The project aims to develop an integrated platform that can help transform environmental and geospatial information into understandable visual outputs that may support researchers, communities, and disaster-management stakeholders.

The initial research focus is Rwanda, with the long-term potential to adapt the approach to other climate-vulnerable regions across Africa.

**Current development stage:** Web-dashboard prototype and Google Earth Engine rainfall-analysis workflow.

**Development year:** 2026.

> **Important:** This is a research prototype, not an operational flood early-warning system. The current dashboard does not provide verified live satellite flood monitoring, a trained and validated AI prediction model, or automatic emergency alerts.

---

## 🎯 2. Project Objectives

The main objective is to investigate how Earth Observation and AI-assisted geospatial analysis can strengthen flood-risk information and disaster preparedness.

Specific objectives include:

1. Explore the use of satellite and environmental datasets for flood-related research.
2. Process rainfall information using Google Earth Engine.
3. Develop a web-based dashboard for presenting flood-related information.
4. Explore geospatial methods for identifying areas that may be exposed to flooding.
5. Investigate future integration of AI and Machine Learning techniques.
6. Support research into accessible disaster-risk information for Rwanda.
7. Establish a foundation for future testing, validation, and development.

## 🌧️ 3. Current Project Components

### A. Web-Based Research Dashboard

The project includes an interactive website designed to present flood-related information and demonstrate an initial observation-assessment workflow.

Current dashboard components include:

- Rwanda reference map.
- Flood observation input form.
- Rainfall-condition selection.
- River and water-level observations.
- Flood-status observations.
- Exposure information.
- Observation-based screening summary.
- Safety guidance and prototype limitations.

The assessment function summarizes information entered by the user. It does not calculate a scientifically validated flood probability or replace an official warning.

### B. Google Earth Engine Rainfall Analysis

Google Earth Engine is used as a geospatial processing environment.

The current working research script uses the **ERA5-Land Hourly** dataset to explore rainfall information.

The workflow includes:

- Accessing hourly rainfall data.
- Processing a 24-hour period.
- Calculating rainfall information.
- Displaying rainfall results.
- Presenting hourly rainfall information in a chart or table.
- Creating a rainfall visualization.

**Open the Google Earth Engine research script:**

https://code.earthengine.google.com/f1972a844db1d1d049689064a2e9fa41

Access may require a Google account with the appropriate Earth Engine permissions.

## ⚠️ 13. Limitations and Responsible Use

This project is a research and technology prototype under development.

Its current limitations include:

- The dashboard is not connected to a verified live satellite flood-monitoring service.
- The observation-assessment function depends on information entered by the user.
- The current rainfall workflow does not, by itself, establish that flooding is occurring at a particular location.
- A trained and validated AI/ML flood-prediction model has not yet been implemented.
- Risk thresholds and analytical methods require further research and validation.
- Field observations and historical flood-event data are needed for stronger evaluation.

**The dashboard must not be treated as an official emergency-warning service.** For emergencies, users should follow information and instructions from the relevant authorities.
